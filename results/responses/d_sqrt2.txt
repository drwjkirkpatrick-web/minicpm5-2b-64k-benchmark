To prove that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational, we will use a classic proof by contradiction. This method assumes that $\sqrt{2}$ is rational and then shows that this assumption leads to a logical contradiction.

### Step 1: Assume $\sqrt{2}$ is Rational
Assume that $\sqrt{2}$ is a rational number. By definition, this means that $\sqrt{2}$ can be expressed as a fraction $\frac{p}{q}$, where:
1. $p$ and $q$ are integers.
2. $q \neq 0$.
3. The fraction is in its simplest form (i.e., $p$ and $q$ have no common factors other than 1).

So, we can write:
$$ \sqrt{2} = \frac{p}{q} $$

### Step 2: Square Both Sides
To eliminate the square root and work with integers, square both sides of the equation:
$$ (\sqrt{2})^2 = \left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^2 $$
$$ 2 = \frac{p^2}{q^2} $$

### Step 3: Rearrange to isolate integers
Multiply both sides by $q^2$ to clear the denominator:
$$ 2q^2 = p^2 $$

This equation tells us that $p^2$ is an even number (since it is equal to $2$ times $q^2$).

### Step 4: Use the Property of Even Numbers
A fundamental property of integers is that if a square number is even, then its root must also be even.
*   **Proof of property:** If $n$ is an integer and $n^2$ is even, then $n$ must be even. If $n$ were odd, we could write $n = 2k + 1$. Then $n^2 = (2k+1)^2 = 4k^2 + 4k + 1 = 2(2k^2 + 2k) + 1$, which is odd. Since $n^2$ is even, $n$ cannot be odd.

Since $p^2$ is even, **$p$ must be even**.

### Step 5: Express $p$ using an integer $k$
Because $p$ is even, there exists an integer $k$ such that:
$$ p = 2k $$

### Step 6: Substitute back into the original equation
Substitute $p = 2k$ back into the equation from Step 3 ($2q^2 = p^2$):
$$ 2q^2 = (2k)^2 $$
$$ 2q^2 = 4k^2 $$

Divide both sides by 2:
$$ q^2 = 2k^2 $$

### Step 7: Analyze $q$
The equation $q^2 = 2k^2$ implies that $q^2$ is also even (specifically, it is 2 times $k^2$). By the same logic used in Step 4, if $q^2$ is even, then **$q$ must also be even**.

### Step 8: Identify the Contradiction
We have established two facts:
1.  $p$ is even.
2.  $q$ is even.

However, recall the initial assumption in Step 1 that $\frac{p}{q}$ was in **simplest form** (i.e., $p$ and $q$ share no common factors). If both $p$ and $q$ are even, they are both divisible by 2. This means we can divide both by 2, which contradicts the assumption that the fraction was in simplest form.

Alternatively, we can look at the ratio:
$$ \frac{p}{q} = \frac{2k}{2m} = \frac{k}{m} $$
This implies that $\sqrt{2} = \frac{k}{m}$, which is a new fraction with smaller integers than $\frac{p}{q}$. This contradicts the idea that we started with the simplest form of the fraction representing $\sqrt{2}$.

### Conclusion
The assumption that $\sqrt{2}$ is rational leads to a contradiction. Therefore, the assumption must be false.

**Thus, $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.**
